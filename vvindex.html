The VV Index.

Behind the Numbers
One number to hold a whole season, so the greatest careers, across every era, can finally stand against one another. No favourites, no nostalgia, no hiding place. Only what was earned.
13 of 14

Ballon d’Or seasons the Index puts in its top three bands.

It has never seen an award. No honour, team or individual, adds a single point to a VV Score, and a Ballon d’Or adds nothing at all. Thirteen of the fourteen land at 85 or better anyway, and 10 of them at 90.

What it gets right

The Index does not know who these players are. It reads minutes, output, the strength of the league and the position played, and nothing else. The names arrive afterwards, and they are how we check the scale rather than how we set it.

10
Of those fourteen reach 90 or better, and three sit inside the twelve highest-scoring seasons in the entire record. Nothing was tuned to put them there , the band edges are ranks, so no season can be moved into place without moving every season around it.
70
Luka Modrić’s 2017/18, the season that won the Ballon d’Or, and the one the Index misses. Two things are true and we publish both. That award leaned heavily on a third straight Champions League, which is a team outcome the Index refuses to count. And the Index under-reads deep midfielders: it sees the pass completed, not the position taken that made the pass available. One of those is a choice. The other is a limit.

What one number holds

A season, scored against the seasons it should be compared with. Not a career, not a reputation, and not a mark out of ten.

54,416
Seasons carry a VV Score, across nine leagues and sixteen years. The same basis for every one of them, which is the whole reason a 2011 season and a 2025 one can be set against each other at all.
12
Seasons have ever scored 95. Rarity is the point: 150 reach 90, 650 reach 85, and everything else sits below. A score is a standing among all of them rather than a grade.
Ownpool
Every season is read against players in the same position, never against the whole record. A centre-back is measured among centre-backs. That is why a defender’s number is not a striker’s number made smaller.
9
Leagues, and they are not treated as equal. The weighting is computed from players who actually moved between them, not assigned by reputation.

What it will not count

Not gaps in the record. Decisions, and this is the one that shapes every score on the platform.

Zero
The points a trophy adds to a VV Score. Honours sit beside the number and never inside it. A medal is a team's outcome with one player's name on it, and the Index scores the season he played, not the one his club had. Individual awards are no different , the score is finished before any honour is even joined to it.

What the number chose

Not a limit. A decision, and one that shapes every score on the platform.

Attackthe lean
The scale tilts toward attacking output, because that is where the evidence is , goals and assists are recorded everywhere and back to 2010, and defending is not. We say so rather than claiming a balance the record cannot support.

What the number cannot see

Two limits that no amount of data collection fixes, because they are about what football records, not about what we gathered.

Halfof the job
The game records what a defender does far more faithfully than what he prevents. The tackle is counted. The run that made the tackle unnecessary is not, and neither is the pass a striker never attempted because the space was gone.

So the best defenders score lower here than their reputation, and we are not going to fix that by inventing a number. Van Dijk sits in the low seventies, and we publish that with the reason rather than quietly moving him. A score that agrees with every reputation is not measuring reputation, it is copying it.
Topof the ladder
Positions are verified by hand near the top of the ladder and get coarser below it. A high score is a claim we have checked. A low one is an estimate.

Goalkeepers

The five dimensions above are outfield measures. A keeper is read on a different set, and his card says so.
What We Measure
Save percentageOf the shots that reached his goal, the share he kept out.⌄

It is the one number on a keeper’s card that is his own work rather than his team’s, and it is the only thing his ranking is built from.

A keeper needs a real season behind him before we will rank it: 800 minutes and 60 shots faced. Below either, the card says he was not scored and shows what was recorded instead. A rate over a handful of shots is noise wearing a decimal point.

He is ranked against keepers, and nobody elseA save rate means nothing set beside a striker’s numbers.⌄

The ladder places a keeper against every other keeper we can measure, which is what makes the percentile mean something. Ranking him in the general population would be arithmetic without a question behind it.

That pool starts in 2015, because that is when shot data starts. Before it the record holds his minutes and his starts and nothing more, and the card says so rather than filling the gap with a guess.

Where We Cannot See
Shots faced is context, not creditHow busy a keeper was is a fact about the team in front of him.⌄

A keeper behind a poor defence faces more shots and makes more saves, and neither fact says he kept goal well. Volume tracks the weakness of the side, near enough one for one.

So the saved-and-conceded bar is drawn plainly, in ink rather than in the colour the score uses, and the number of shots sits beneath it as context. It is there to tell you what kind of season it was, never to argue that it was a good one.

Penalties saved is a fact, not a scoreWe count them. We cannot count how many he faced.⌄

Stopping a penalty is genuinely the keeper’s own act, so it is stated on his card. But a count with no denominator is not a rate, and scoring it would reward whoever happened to face the most.

So it stays a stated figure and is kept out of the number. Tested as a scoring term it promoted keepers with a lucky night and demoted the best save rate in the record, which is the whole argument against it.

We record whether a shot was saved, never how hard it wasTwenty close-range chances and twenty from distance score the same here.⌄

There is nothing in the record that tells a tap-in from a top corner, so save percentage separates keepers partially and not fully. We would rather say that than let the rate imply a precision it does not have.

Nor does anything here measure distribution, command of the area or sweeping. A keeper who reads danger before it arrives and one who waits on his line are, to us, the same keeper. That is a limit of the record, not a judgement on either of them.

And a keeper carries no score at allA keeper’s card shows no VV Score, because the Index cannot yet earn one.⌄

The VV Index does not yet weigh goalkeeping. The engine still holds a number for every keeper, capped at 75 and built from minutes played and the strength of his league rather than from anything he did with his hands.

That is why no card shows it. A number that cannot move would read as a verdict on the keeper rather than as a boundary of the platform, and it would sit directly above a save-rate ladder measuring something else entirely. What his card carries instead is his save rate against other keepers, and the figures it was built from.

The held number has not stopped working, though, and one place still shows its effect: keepers appear in ranked lists in the order it gives them, while no card prints it. The ordering is visible and the number behind it is not, so it is named here rather than left for a reader to infer. Lifting the cap is a change to the engine, not to the card.

Where the ground still moves

A score is a standing, not a mark out of ten, so it answers to every season measured beside it.

878cards
Sit within a single point of a band edge , 1.61% of every scored season. They are the ones a growing record can move.

We changed how defenders are scored. 10,770 seasons moved. Not one crossed a band, and we are telling you anyway.

The defensive signal was two measures blended into one number. It is one measure now, and the biggest single mover was Azpilicueta's 2018, from 70 to 72.
