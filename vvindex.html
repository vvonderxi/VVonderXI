Behind the Numbers
One number to hold a whole season, so the greatest careers, across every era, can finally stand against one another. No favourites, no nostalgia, no hiding place. Only what was earned.
VV
91

What the number means

The VV Index distils a season into a single score, and the higher it climbs, the rarer the season. It is not a popularity contest or a goal tally , it is a weighted read of everything a player did, judged against the demands of their position. The higher the number, the rarer the season.

The Five Dimensions
Tap each point to read the dimension
How much a player threatens the goal , shots, conversion, and the danger they carry in the box.
This is the cleanest dimension to read and the easiest to misjudge. Goal threat is not the same as goals scored. We measure the quality and volume of the chances a player gets into, how often they take them, and the gravity they exert in the penalty area , the defenders pulled out of shape simply because of where they stand. A poacher with ten tap-ins and a forward who drags two centre-halves with every run are not the same player, and the Index knows it.
The chances a player makes for others , key passes, assists, and the moments that unlock a defence.
Assists flatter and deceive in equal measure; a player can lay on twenty and create little, or create relentlessly and finish a season with five to show for it. So we look past the final ball to the chance itself , its danger, its difficulty, the lock it picked. Creation rewards the pass that should have been a goal as much as the one that was. It is the truest measure of a player who makes others better.
How a player moves the ball and the team up the pitch , carries, line-breaking passes, territory gained.
Football is a territorial game, and somebody has to do the carrying. Progression captures the unglamorous, decisive work of advancing the ball through the thirds , the carry that beats the press, the pass that splits a line, the yards won that turn defence into attack. It is why a deep midfielder with no goals and no assists can still be the engine of everything, and why the Index refuses to let that work go unseen.
The work without the ball , duels, recoveries, interceptions, and the protection a player gives the side.
The half of the game that never makes the highlight reel. Defensive value is read in context , a full-back is asked for different things than a holding midfielder, and both are judged against their own brief, not a single blunt standard. Recoveries, duels won, the interception that ends an attack before it starts, the positional discipline that means the tackle was never needed. The best defending often looks like nothing happening at all.
Consistency and availability , minutes played, error rate, and how dependable the season was as a whole.
Talent that cannot be counted on is talent half-spent. Reliability is the season-long view , the minutes actually played, the errors that cost, the weeks a player was there when the team needed him versus the weeks he was not. A brilliant half-season and a steady full one are different achievements, and this is the dimension that tells them apart. Availability, in the end, is a skill of its own.
Goalkeepers
The five dimensions above are outfield measures. A keeper is read on a different set, and his card says so.
What We Measure
Save percentageOf the shots that reached his goal, the share he kept out.⌄

It is the one number on a keeper’s card that is his own work rather than his team’s, and it is the only thing his ranking is built from.

A keeper needs a real season behind him before we will rank it: 800 minutes and 60 shots faced. Below either, the card says he was not scored and shows what was recorded instead. A rate over a handful of shots is noise wearing a decimal point.

He is ranked against keepers, and nobody elseA save rate means nothing set beside a striker’s numbers.⌄

The ladder places a keeper against every other keeper we can measure, which is what makes the percentile mean something. Ranking him in the general population would be arithmetic without a question behind it.

That pool starts in 2015, because that is when shot data starts. Before it the record holds his minutes and his starts and nothing more, and the card says so rather than filling the gap with a guess.

Where We Cannot See
Shots faced is context, not creditHow busy a keeper was is a fact about the team in front of him.⌄

A keeper behind a poor defence faces more shots and makes more saves, and neither fact says he kept goal well. Volume tracks the weakness of the side, near enough one for one.

So the saved-and-conceded bar is drawn plainly, in ink rather than in the colour the score uses, and the number of shots sits beneath it as context. It is there to tell you what kind of season it was, never to argue that it was a good one.

Penalties saved is a fact, not a scoreWe count them. We cannot count how many he faced.⌄

Stopping a penalty is genuinely the keeper’s own act, so it is stated on his card. But a count with no denominator is not a rate, and scoring it would reward whoever happened to face the most.

So it stays a stated figure and is kept out of the number. Tested as a scoring term it promoted keepers with a lucky night and demoted the best save rate in the record, which is the whole argument against it.

We record whether a shot was saved, never how hard it wasTwenty close-range chances and twenty from distance score the same here.⌄

There is nothing in the record that tells a tap-in from a top corner, so save percentage separates keepers partially and not fully. We would rather say that than let the rate imply a precision it does not have.

Nor does anything here measure distribution, command of the area or sweeping. A keeper who reads danger before it arrives and one who waits on his line are, to us, the same keeper. That is a limit of the record, not a judgement on either of them.

And the score itself is cappedEvery keeper is capped at 75, whatever his save rate.⌄

The VV Index does not yet weigh goalkeeping. Until it does, a keeper’s number reflects availability and the strength of his league, and nothing about how he kept goal.

The cap is printed on the card for that reason. A number that cannot move would otherwise contradict the save-rate ladder directly beneath it, and a reader would take the low score as a verdict on the keeper rather than a boundary of the platform. Lifting it is a change to the engine, not to the card.

The Bands
Generational:95+⌄
The rarest score the Index gives: roughly a dozen seasons in the entire record. Not the best of its year, the best of many, the kind that resets where the ceiling sits. This high, the number has stopped measuring a player against his peers and begun measuring him against history.
Iconic:90+⌄
One tier below Generational: fewer than a hundred and fifty seasons in the whole record. The level most great careers are remembered by, unarguable in its own year without claiming to rewrite the rest. Elite company, and rare, but not the rarest air.
World Class:85+⌄
Among the very best of its year. A player operating at the summit of the global game, the level title races are won at.
Put this season before any jury, in any era, and the answer returns the same. World class is not a claim here. It is a finding.
Standout:80+⌄
A standout campaign. A clear difference-maker who shaped his team's season and would walk into most sides in the league.
Not yet the summit, but unmistakably the work of someone built for the climb. The room has already taken notice.
Accomplished:≤79⌄
A reliable, quality season at a good level. The dependable spine of a competitive team, the player who does his job well week in and week out, rarely spectacular but rarely a liability, and quietly central to almost everything that works.
No headlines, no fuss, no nights off. Ask the dressing room who they trust, and this is the name they say first.
The Method, and Its Limits
What the number does on purpose, and where it cannot see.
What We Measure
A season is ranked against every seasonThe number is a standing in the whole record, not a mark out of ten.⌄

Nothing here is scored against a fixed rubric. Every season is placed against every other season the Index has measured, across all nine leagues and every year in the record at once, which is what lets a 2016 campaign and a 2024 one stand in the same line. The number answers one question: of everything we know, where does this sit.

Position is the lensA centre-half is not a failed striker, and nobody is marked down for the job they were asked to do.⌄

A holding midfielder and a winger are never judged by the same yardstick, and a goalkeeper is no one’s playmaker. Each player is read through the lens that fits the role, every dimension weighted by what that role actually demands. A low goal tally is not a low score. It is the wrong measure, declined.

The league is part of the numberLeague strength is measured from players who actually moved between leagues, never assigned by reputation.⌄

Not every league is the same, and the Index knows it. Strength is read from the players who have actually crossed between them, corrected both for the age a player was when they moved and for how freely that league scores, then solved across all nine leagues at once rather than league by league. It is recomputed season by season, because a league is not a fixed quantity. A quiet fifteen goals in an elite league and a loud fifteen in a weaker one are not the same achievement, and the strength of the competition is part of the score, not a badge beside it.

The unit is a season, not a highlightMinutes are the denominator, so a hot month cannot pass for a great year.⌄

A season is judged whole. Output is read against the time on the pitch that produced it, which is why a player who scores eight in nine hundred minutes and one who scores eight in three thousand are not the same season, and why the Index will not build a card from a fragment. Form is a month. This measures a year.

The scale leans, and we say soThe score tilts toward attacking output, because that is where the evidence is firm.⌄

Two things are true at once, so we will say both. No player is punished for not scoring; goal threat is measured as a dimension, not a raw count, and nobody is marked down for the job they were asked to do. But the Index does not weigh everything equally, because the data does not arrive equally. A goal is recorded the same way everywhere; an interception, a covering run, a line held, is recorded less consistently, and for some seasons not at all. So the score leans harder on attacking output than on defensive work. That is a deliberate tilt, not an oversight. We would rather lean on what we can measure well than invent a precision we do not have.

Nothing reaches 100The scale stops short of perfection, by design.⌄

The very top is compressed deliberately, so no season can claim to be flawless. There is always another level to imagine, and a scale that hands out full marks has stopped being a measurement and started being a compliment.

What We Don’t
Where the record is incompleteWhere a number was never logged, we mark it NR , never zero.⌄

Football’s bookkeeping was not always thorough. For seasons before 2015, and for some competitions beyond Europe’s principal leagues, figures like assists were never logged at source. Where a number was never recorded, we mark it NR. Not Recorded. It is not a zero. A player who created nothing and a player whose creation was never counted are different players, and we will not flatten one into the other.

Where a career is missingSome careers were never linked at source, and we would rather omit than invent.⌄

Some careers are not here. A number of players, particularly from the earlier seasons, have no season-by-season record in our data source, and no amount of searching will surface what was never linked. We could have stitched something together from thinner material and called it a rating. We chose not to. A card you find here is a card we can stand behind; a card we cannot build honestly, we leave out.

Where the sample is too thinBelow roughly three hundred minutes we hold back rather than rate a fragment.⌄

A season needs enough minutes to mean something. Below roughly three hundred, a handful of appearances, we hold back rather than rate a fragment. It means the very edge of every squad sits outside the Index. That is the cost of only scoring what we can score fairly.

Where the defender is hardest to readThe game records what a defender prevents far less faithfully than what an attacker creates.⌄

Defenders are the hardest to rate honestly, because the game records what they prevent far less faithfully than what an attacker creates. The best defending often leaves no trace at all, the tackle not needed, the run cut out before it began, while a mistimed lunge shows up in the numbers. So the data can undercount the very defenders who read the game best.

And defensive numbers depend on the team around them. A defender in a dominant side faces few situations and posts low volumes; a defender in a besieged one racks up tackles and clearances precisely because his team concedes so much. A high tackle count can mark a worse defensive context, not a better defender, context distorts these numbers in a way it never distorts a goal. So we score defenders in their own company, against other defenders, and tell you plainly where the data thins, rather than dressing a gap as a judgment.

Where the position is certain, and where it is notPositions are verified by hand near the top of the ladder, and coarser further down.⌄

A season is scored inside a position, because a full-back and a striker are not doing the same job. Near the top of the ladder those positions are verified by hand, season by season, against the role a player actually occupied that year.

Further down they are coarser. We know a player was a defender, not that he was a full-back, and a season measured inside a wider field is measured less precisely.

So a score near the top is a claim we have checked. A score in the tail is an estimate, and we would rather say so than let it pass for more.

Where a penalty is only counted from 2015Penalties are in the record from 2015, so a taker before then is scored on his total goals alone.⌄

A penalty is a goal, and it counts. It is not quite the same feat as the others, though, so the Index weighs it a little under a full goal. A season built on spot-kicks and a season built on open play should not read identically.

That is only possible where the record says which goals were penalties, and it says so from 2015. Before then the column is empty, not zero, and we will not guess which goals were which.

So a penalty taker from an earlier season is scored on his total, and reads a little higher than the same season would today. The alternative is to treat an empty column as a player who never took one, and that is not a silence we are willing to fill in on his behalf.

Where the number falls silentNo statistic counts an absence, so where the number goes quiet, the verdict speaks.⌄

No statistic yet invented can count an absence, and some of a season’s decisive moments are absences, the pass not attempted, the mistake never made. Where the number falls quiet, the verdict speaks: what the score cannot see, the writing will tell you.

Where the ground still movesA score is a standing among every season measured, so it moves as that record grows.⌄

A VV Score is not a grade stamped once and left alone. It is a season’s standing against every other season the Index has measured, and that record keeps growing, so the number can move. It moves for two reasons, and both of them are the Index getting truer.

The first is completeness. Football’s bookkeeping has gaps, and when we recover seasons that were missing, the field of comparison fills in around every card already standing in it. A card that read 85 last month may read 84 today, not because the football changed, but because the company it is measured against grew more complete.

The second is the benchmark itself. The bar for the very best is set by the best there has ever been, and that company grows. A great season from years ago can move as new greatness arrives to stand beside it, because we do not freeze the top of the scale around whatever we happened to have measured first.

We think this is the honest way. A score should answer how does this rank among everything we know, not sit frozen against a smaller world it happened to be measured in first. We would rather a number move toward the truth than hold still and slowly become a lie.

A record you can trust is worth more than a record that is merely full.
