What to look for, in order

1. Does D look like a tint or a stain? 1.14 against cream is the whole risk. If the wash reads as discolouration rather than as a deliberate highlight, D is finished regardless of its 7.00.

2. Does D match the reference? Put the card column and the Compare column side by side and ask whether a reader meeting both in one session sees one treatment. That is the entire argument for D over B , the ratios already favour neither.

3. D IS NOW GRACEFUL, WHICH WAS THE OPEN QUESTION AND IT HAS A CLEAN ANSWER. The first cut used #5C4008 and dropped to 1.27 against scout's body ink, so a wash that failed to render took the emphasis with it. #7e5a10 clears AA ON THE WASH at 4.57 and still reads as emphasis WITHOUT it , 5.21 on bare cream, 1.94 / 2.75 separation, which is exactly what ships today as option A. D is now A plus a block, and it degrades to A.

AND THE WASH IS THE BINDING GROUND, NOT THE CREAM. The wash is DARKER than the paper, so contrast on it is always the lower of the two , satisfy AA there and the bare-cream fallback is free. On the wash, scout's body sits at 8.87, so the best separation any ink can reach while clearing AA is 1.97. #7e5a10 reaches 1.94. There is 0.03 left in the entire colour space, which is the same reciprocal wall as the light page, one ground further in.

4. Scout is the hard case either way. Its body ink is #3A352E and italic, so it is already emphatic; B buys it 2.21 and D changes its texture rather than its contrast. If the two boxes disagree about which option wins, say so , they can take different answers, and the notes block is the one that carries most of the prose.

Not in this demo, deliberately: glDrury. Its body ink is #5F594E, 5.78 on cream, so the best separation available at AA is 1.28. That is a measurement, not a preference. It takes no emphasis and none of these options changes that.

If D is chosen

Three declarations scattered across three files is the drift this codebase keeps recording; three grounds honestly served in one place is not duplication.
