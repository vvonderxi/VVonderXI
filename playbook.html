≤79
Accomplished, the honest backbone of the professional game.
Every team needs them. The dependable, useful seasons that keep a side ticking over, the work that rarely makes the montage but always makes the difference. This is the broad base of the professional game and the vast majority of all seasons. The Index rates every one of them with the same honesty it gives the summit.
How it's built
Goals and assists first, the numbers fans remember. Then shaped by position (a striker is judged differently from a defender), and adjusted for league strength, so a season in a tougher division counts for more. The result: one fair number that lets any two seasons, from any of the 9 leagues, stand side by side.
100 is unreachable by design. The very top is reserved, the ceiling exists so greatness always has somewhere left to climb.
The bands are named levels, not precise cuts. A season near a band edge could sit either side of it. The number is measured more finely than the names divide it, so a band is the neighbourhood a season is in, not a line it cleared.
The VV Score is not a popularity contest, and not a sum of trophies. It measures a single season's output, weighed against the standard of its position and the strength of its stage. A striker is asked for goals; a playmaker, for creation; a defender, for control, each judged against their own kind. Then the league is accounted for, because thirty goals in the strongest division is not thirty goals in a gentler one. What remains is a single, defensible figure, the closest thing the game has to a common currency, so any two seasons, separated by decades or borders, can finally be set on the same table and asked the only question that matters: who was greater?
The Foundation

🕸️ The Five Dimensions

Every player is read across the same five dimensions, so any card compares to any other. The shape tells you the type.
Goal Threat The danger a player carries in and around the box , the chances they get into, how often they take them, and the defenders pulled out of shape simply by where they stand. It is not the same as goals scored: a poacher with ten tap-ins and a forward who drags two centre-halves with every run are not the same player.
Creation The chances a player makes for others, judged by the chance itself , its danger, its difficulty, the lock it picked , rather than by whether someone finished it. Assists flatter and deceive in equal measure, so Creation rewards the pass that should have been a goal as fully as the one that was.
Progression The work of moving the ball and the team up the pitch: the carry that beats the press, the pass that splits a line, the yards that turn defence into attack. Football is a territorial game and somebody has to do the carrying, which is why a deep midfielder with no goals and no assists can still be the engine of everything.
Defensive The half of the game that never makes the highlight reel , duels, recoveries, interceptions, and the positional discipline that means the tackle was never needed. It is read in context, a full-back and a holding midfielder judged against their own brief rather than one blunt standard, because the best defending often looks like nothing happening at all.
Reliability The season-long view: minutes actually played, errors that cost, and whether a player was there in the weeks the team needed him. Talent that cannot be counted on is talent half-spent, and a brilliant half-season and a steady full one are different achievements.
Five numbers, but never only numbers. They are the shape of a player , the fingerprint no two careers share. One man lives for the goal; another for the pass that breathes it into being. One carries the ball forward like a rising tide; another holds the line when the night turns cold. Reduce a season to these five and you do not shrink it , you reveal it. This is where the score finds its muscle and its meaning: not in a single figure handed down from on high, but in the living, breathing shape behind it , the truth of how a footballer spent his year.

Every keeper is capped at 75.
The VV Index does not yet weigh goalkeeping, so no save rate can move the number. The fourteenth best save season we hold scores the same as the thousandth, and de Gea's 17/18 is that fourteenth. The cap is stated on the card, because a number that cannot move should say it cannot move.
Every one of those marks is the same size, and that is not a design choice.
We record whether a shot was saved, never how hard it was. A tap-in in the six-yard box and a thirty-yard flying save both count one. The score cannot weigh them, so the card says so rather than implying a precision it does not have.

A save rate means nothing against a striker's numbers, so a keeper is ranked against every other keeper we can measure and against nobody else.

There is no radar

Not one of the five dimensions asks about keeping goal. The pentagon is not hidden, it is inapplicable, so the card puts it away and shows the marks instead.

Shots faced is the defence

Facing more shots earns a keeper nothing. How many arrive describes the team in front of him, not the man behind them, so it frames his rate and never enters his score. De Gea's 143 was busier than most, the 68th percentile of the seasons here, and that is context rather than credit.

Penalties saved is a fact, not a score

Stopping one is genuinely his own act and we count it. How many he faced is not recorded, so there is no rate to be had and none is invented.

Drawn only when the season can carry it: 800 minutes, 60 shots faced, 2015 onward. 1,920 of 2,806 keeper seasons with save data qualify, and every one that does not says which gate it missed. Seasons with no save recorded are not counted at all, because they were never eligible to fail a gate.

The Method

📊 What "percentile" means

A measure of rarity, not just volume.
When a card says a player is in the 95th percentile for creation, it means only 5 in 100 players in their position and league created more that season. It ranks them against their real peers, in their real context.

The Method

🔎 What "Data Confidence" means

How complete the record is. Not how good the player was.
Every card carries a five-dot confidence mark. Five dots means a fully documented season, every match logged, every touch counted. Fewer dots means we had less to work with, and we would rather show you that than pretend otherwise. It measures the record, not the player. A three-dot card is not a worse footballer, it is a season we know a little less about. Where a single statistic was never recorded, you will see NR , Not Recorded , in its place. Older seasons and some leagues never logged assists or detailed actions at all. We mark those as NR rather than write a zero, because a missing number and a number of zero are not the same thing, and we will not let one masquerade as the other.
"Every number here is only as honest as the evidence beneath it. Some seasons left a full and faithful record; others, older or more distant, gave us only fragments, and where the trail runs thin, we will not invent what we cannot see. These five dots are our conscience made visible: the confidence we show is the confidence we have earned, and never a dot more."
