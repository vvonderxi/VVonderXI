The Playbook goalkeeper section , three ways to make it show rather than tell

Today: 522 words, zero graphics, third of eleven sections. Every claim in it is visual and none of them is drawn. Each direction below keeps the same five claims and replaces prose with a figure. Word counts are of the replacement copy.

D1 , swap the radar
Show the thing being put away

~110 words. One figure. Leads on the section's actual subject: a keeper card is the outfield card with the radar removed and something else in its place.

The pentagon struck through, the keeper read beside it. The sentence "a keeper's card puts the radar away" stops being a claim and becomes the picture.

Keeps: what replaces the radar, the 800/60 gate, keepers-only percentile. Cuts: the paragraph explaining that the five dimensions ask outfield questions , the crossed pentagon says it.

D3 , lead with the limit
The honest bit first

~120 words. Two figures. Opens on what the score cannot see, which is the section's real argument and the platform's voice elsewhere.

The published limitation, drawn. Same register as the Index's "Where We Cannot See".

Then the score, the gate, and the keepers-only ladder in three short lines under it.

Risk: opening on a limit reads as apology if the figure is not confident. Needs the strongest drawing of the three.

All three keep the section's five claims. D2 cuts the most words; D1 is the most legible at a glance; D3 is the most in the platform's voice. None changes the ordering , the section is still third of eleven, which is a separate question about whether a keeper explainer belongs above the tag library.
