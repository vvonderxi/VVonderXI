Tier Guide▲
S-Tier30+
Ideal: 25G+8A / 15G+18A
Generational. Only the most dominant players in world football sustain this level of output across a full season. Titles are won around these numbers — and careers are defined by them. You are watching history being made.
Elite25–30
Ideal: 20G+7A / 10G+17A
World-class output. Top-5 globally in their position and every serious club knows it. The system is built around this player, and the transfer fee reflects that — this is the profile that wins titles and unlocks trophies.
Excellent20–25
Ideal: 18G+5A / 8G+14A
Champions League asset. A reliable double-digit contributor every season who makes a tangible difference to results week after week. Scouts at the biggest clubs have a full dossier on this player already.
Very Good15–20
Ideal: 13G+4A / 5G+12A
Consistent starter at a high level who delivers without drama every single week. In a weaker league, this is a standout performer being watched closely. In a top league, this is a player building towards something bigger.
Good10–15
Ideal: 10G+3A / 4G+8A
Dependable squad player who contributes without being the headline act. The key question a scout asks here is simple: is this the ceiling, or just the foundation? Age, trajectory, and context will tell you everything.
Okay5–10
Ideal: 7G+1A / 2G+6A
Functional output that keeps a player in the team without turning heads. Acceptable for a defensive midfielder or fullback who adds value elsewhere — but for an attacker, this demands a serious conversation about whether they belong at this level.
Poor0–5
Minimum: 3G+1A
Below expected output by any standard. A scout filing a report on these numbers needs a compelling explanation — injury, wrong position, broken system — before they can recommend any investment. The burden of proof is high.
Label Guide▲
Not Even Close
A dominant gap of 8+ league-adjusted G+A. One player is in a different class — no debate needed on the numbers alone.
Clear Advantage
A gap of 5–7 adjusted contributions. Meaningful and hard to argue against. The stronger player is evident on output metrics.
Edges It
A gap of 3–4 adjusted G+A. The better player is identifiable but the margin is close enough that context could shift the conclusion.
Tight Call
Only 1–2 adjusted contributions separate these players. Both are credible options — age, contract, and system fit become decisive.
VAR Call
A statistical dead heat. The numbers alone cannot split them. A scout would need footage, physical data, and deeper context before filing a recommendation.
League Gap
The two players are competing in leagues with a significant UEFA coefficient difference. Raw numbers must be discounted accordingly — context is everything here.
Age Advantage
One player is 3+ years younger with comparable or better output. That gap in trajectory, potential, and resale value is a major factor in any recruitment decision.
Role Difference
These players operate in fundamentally different positions. Direct G+A comparison without positional context is misleading — output expectations vary significantly by role.
Breakout Season
A league-adjusted improvement of 8+ G+A from the prior season. This player has taken a significant step up — the kind of curve that triggers transfer interest.
Decline Flag
A league-adjusted drop of 8+ G+A from the prior season. Could be injury, form, or the start of a natural decline. A scout would investigate before committing.
