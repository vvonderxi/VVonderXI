The league strip , revision demo

Same three lines of facts in every treatment. What changes is whether the drawing is carrying the data or carrying the brand.

The measurement first, because it decides the rest

The wedges are to scale. Serie A is 44.53°, Belgium is 35.95°, against a perfectly equal 40.00° , a 1.239 ratio end to end. But the two extremes sit on opposite sides of the ring and can never be compared directly, and the median gap between two neighbouring leagues is 0.88°, which is 1.14px of arc at 168px and 0.90px at 132px. That is a sub-pixel difference on the only comparison a reader can actually make. The chart is to scale and unreadable at the same time. That is the chart failing, not the eye.

A , the ring kept, with each league's own colours

True brand colours instead of the arbitrary palette, and a percentage on every chip. Note what the real colours do: four of the nine leagues are red (Bundesliga, Belgium, Turkey, and La Liga's red-orange) and two are navy (Serie A, Ligue 1). Real colours are less separable than invented ones, so colour cannot be the cue here , the flag already is.

B , the object. A football, and the numbers where numbers work

No data ring. A drawn football in the platform's own icon language, and the count and the share live on the chip and in the popover, where a reader can actually read them. Nothing is lost: the ring never communicated the 1.24 ratio to anybody.

C , the strip. The only treatment where the 1.24 ratio is legible

Length against a common baseline is the one channel that survives a 1.24 ratio, and the pale line marks a perfectly equal share, so "they are all the same size" becomes something you can see rather than something we assert. The ball stays as the object, small, beside the heading.
