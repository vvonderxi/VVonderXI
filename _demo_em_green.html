Before aesthetics: CLAUDE.md settles this one

Under-the-Lights green is Compare-only by locked rule , "Compare headings and finding boxes. Standard tone everywhere else, do NOT bleed the green into card, rankings, playbook."

Emphasis is not a Compare feature. .vvem renders on the card's Scout Report and Commentator's Notes as well. So green emphasis is precisely the bleed that rule exists to prevent, and it would arrive on the card by a route the rule did not anticipate rather than by anyone deciding to put it there.

Two supporting points, in case the rule is ever revisited. Green next to green: Compare's finding boxes are green panels, so green emphasis inside and beside them loses the thing emphasis is for. The link question: the platform's only coloured inline ink on light is --pink-ink #AD0332 and there is no blue inline text anywhere, so nothing here trains a reader to read colour as a link. Green reads as valid or correct, which is its own problem on a page making claims. Gold reads as neither, because gold is already the emphasis colour in dark and the light value is continuous with something the reader has met.

What I would take

Keep gold on light. The wash helps in light and hurts in dark, and I had that wrong. Measured in this page rather than quoted: light ink-on-wash is 7.74 against 4.42 ink-only on the same panel, a real gain. Dark ink-on-wash is 7.45 against 10.51 ink-only, so in dark the wash COSTS contrast. An earlier note of mine put dark at 7.13 and light at 7.74 as though they were the same treatment winning twice. They are not.

And the light gain is not the wall being beaten, it is the wall being sidestepped. The wash cell's ink is #5C4008, not #9A6B00 , far darker than anything that could work as emphasis on white, where it would read as near-black. The proof is in the same row: that ink measures 1.37 against the body ink, which is to say it is very nearly the same darkness as the text around it and would be invisible as emphasis on its own. It works here because the wash carries the signal and the ink no longer has to. That is the real argument for a wash, and it is stronger than the contrast number: emphasis stops being a thing you READ and becomes a thing you SEE, which is why wash-against-page at 1.24 is correct rather than a failure.

So the wash is a two-theme decision, not one treatment. If it is taken, dark keeps ink-only and light takes the wash, or dark takes a wash tuned to gain rather than lose. Do not take one wash for both on the strength of the light cell.

The thing the numbers cannot settle: the wash gives every marked run a visible block, so a paragraph with four marks reads as striped. Cells 3 carry two marks each, the low end of what rule 4 asks for. Look at density, not at the ratio.
