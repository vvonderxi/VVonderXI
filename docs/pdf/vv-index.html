What the VV Index is, in one page

It scores one season. Not a career, not a player, not a transfer value , one season of one player, against every other season we hold.

Most football numbers answer "how many". The Index answers a narrower question: given what this player produced, in this role, in this league, how rare was that season? A striker's twenty goals and a centre-back's season are not compared directly. Each is placed against the seasons of players doing the same job, and the score is where it lands in that company.

That choice has a consequence worth stating early. The Index is comparative, not absolute. A score of 85 does not mean a season passed a bar; it means it stood in a particular place among the seasons we can measure. Add seasons and the ground moves , page 7 is about exactly how much.

A player must clear a minutes floor to enter the record at all, so a squad contributes roughly twenty players a season rather than its full list. That floor is the reason these counts sit near 400 and not near 600.

Covered does not mean equally detailed

Detailed statistics , shots, passes, duels, dribbles , begin in 2015/16, with the Premier League partly from 2014/15 and Belgium from 2020/21. Before those dates the record holds appearances, minutes, goals and assists, and nothing finer.

The five dimensions

Every season is read across the same five, so any card compares to any other. The shape tells you the type of player as much as the score tells you the level.

DimensionWhat it counts
Goal ThreatThe danger carried in and around the box , the chances got into, and what was done with them. Not the same as goals scored.
CreationThe chances made for others, judged at the chance rather than at the finish, because assists flatter and deceive in equal measure.
ProgressionMoving the ball from where it is safe to where it is dangerous, by carry or by pass.
DefensiveThe work without the ball, measured as a share of what the team had to do rather than as a raw count.
ReliabilityHow much of the season the player was actually there for, and how steady it was.

Each dimension is a percentile against other seasons in the same position pool, not an absolute rate. That is why a centre-back and a striker can both show a full defensive spoke without contradiction: they are being read against their own peers, and against nobody else.

What it chose

Several decisions could reasonably have gone the other way. They are listed because a method is only checkable if its choices are visible.

The choiceWhy
One score per season, not per careerA career average hides the thing most worth looking at, which is the season that stands out from it.
Compared across leagues, not withinCross-league comparability is the product. Partitioning by league would make every score local and answer a smaller question.
Defensive work as a shareRaw counts measure the team's trouble, not the player's ability.
Missing is missing, never zeroA season with no assist recorded is not a season with no assists. Absent measures are shown as NR and are never averaged as nought.
No goalkeeping model, rather than a bad oneSaves without a denominator cannot be scored fairly, so the platform says so instead of implying a number.

What it cannot see

This is the longest section in the document, and deliberately so.

A third of the record has no detailed statistics at all

18,725 seasons , 32.8% of everything we hold , carry none of shots, passes, duels or dribbles. 21,447 carry no shot data. Those seasons are scored on what exists and the card says so; they are not scored as though the missing measures were zero.

It does not weigh goalkeeping

A keeper's score is a function of minutes played and league strength. No save rate can move it. Keeper seasons are capped and the card does not print a score it cannot defend.

Defenders are measured by share, not by class

A defender in a side under constant pressure will out-tackle a better defender in a side that keeps the ball. The Index measures share of the team's defensive work, which is honest and still not the same as quality. It does not claim to rank centre-backs.

It cannot say a player filled two roles

A season resolves to exactly one position. A midfielder who split a campaign evenly between two jobs is recorded as one of them and compared against that pool alone. The record has no way to say "both", and the score inherits that.

Some mid-season transfers are held as half a season

Where a player moved between two clubs in the same league, the record sometimes keeps only one half of the year. The card is then honest about what it holds and wrong about the season as a whole, and we would rather say so than quietly sum two halves we have not verified.

It sees no video, and no context

Injuries, managers, systems, a January move, a father's illness. None of it is in the record. A number that fell has a reason and the Index does not know it.

How much the ground moves

The Index is comparative, so adding seasons changes existing scores. Here is the size of that effect, measured rather than reassured about.

872
seasons sit within a single point of a band edge
1.61%
of all scored seasons

A season one point below a boundary can cross it when the population it is measured against changes , when a new league-season is ingested, or a position is corrected. That is a property of a comparative measure, not a fault in it, and the alternative , freezing the scale , would make the number less true over time rather than more.

The bands are evenly spaced. The precision is not.

The error on a score grows roughly sixfold from the top of the ladder to the bottom, while every band is five points wide. The consequence is stated plainly: only the top pair of bands separates at conventional confidence. Two seasons a band apart in the middle of the ladder are not reliably different, and the platform will not pretend they are.

This is why a comparison of two close seasons on the site refuses to crown one. The number is not always able to separate them, and saying so is more useful than a coin flip with a rosette on it.
