**Q1: the separation is right, and the sharpest way to say why is that the two concepts already exist on the platform , they just live in one field, fighting.** A season honour answers "what happened here"; a cabinet answers "who was this man by then." Those are a season fact and a career-to-date fact, and the current model made an exception for exactly one honour (the World Cup) rather than admit the second category exists. This project has been resolving misdescriptions all week, and this is the honours layer's version of one: the World Cup career leg was the cabinet trying to be born through a special case. Two clean concepts beat one concept plus one exception, every time. One necessary tightening for a platform whose atom is the player-season: the cabinet is not just "everything before this season" , it is **everything up to a hard as-of date, and each card's cabinet is frozen at that date forever.** Ronaldo's 2019 card carries the cabinet as it stood in 2019, permanently. Anything later is not history yet on that card. That rule makes the cabinet a season-level fact after all , the state of a career at a moment , which is why it belongs on this platform and would not if it were a live career widget updating retroactively across old cards.

**Q2: what it may assert, and what the verdict may do.** The cabinet asserts dated external facts, verbatim from the record: honour, year, and , inheriting the honours_json distinction that already ships , whether the player or the team won it. It never counts, ranks, or grades: no "5x champion" rollups, no "decorated" adjectives, no ordering of cabinets by weight. The moment cabinets get summarised into sizes, the platform is running an honours scoreboard beside the rt scoreboard, and two scoreboards is exactly the ambient-ranking failure the no-aggregation rule exists to stop. For the verdict: under Path B the critic already may weigh trophies , your commission said trophies are real, never sole , so the cabinet simply becomes payload, symmetric for both cards, as-of each card's own season. The constraint that carries over unchanged is the register one from the named-axis ruling: in a close call the verdict may *report* both cabinets in the balanced "here is what each holds" form, and may weigh them in its argued opinion, but "the cabinet settles it" remains crowning through prose. One genuinely new rule the as-of date creates: **the verdict's knowledge is frozen at the later of the two cards' seasons.** Comparing Ronaldo 2007 against anyone, the critic does not know 2008 is coming. Retrospective knowledge , "he would go on to win five more" , is the platform grading a season by its future, which breaks the atom the same way a live cabinet would.

**Q3: the empty cabinet is a display rule, not a copy problem , the section does not render.** Do not ship an empty box with consoling text ("nothing yet", "the story begins"), because any sentence in an empty cabinet editorialises a void: it either patronises the journeyman or writes destiny for the wonderkid. Absence of the section is the honest statement, exactly as the platform already handles a tag that wasn't earned , there is no "no tags yet" panel either. The Playbook line: *the cabinet appears with its first entry.* That gives the section a small narrative property for free , somewhere in a career's card sequence, a cabinet appears, and that is the record speaking, not the prose.

**Q4: yes, retire the World Cup career leg, and this is my favourite thing about the proposal , it deletes a rule.** The career leg was the lingering answer implemented as an exception; the cabinet is the lingering answer implemented as a category. Two homes for one fact means eventual drift, so: World Cup as a season honour on the season it was won, in the cabinet with its year on every card after, career leg gone. Keep the one honest thing the old design encoded , the year-printed discipline you already ruled for career-legged facts , which the cabinet inherits wholesale, since dated entries were the whole point of that ruling. Migration note: the retirement plus the new section touches tag surfaces and the payload, so it rides a versioned release with the standing fingerprint plan, not a quiet cleanup.

**Placement: glance, not face, not panel.** The face is the season's verdict about itself , rt, band, the season honours , and putting the cabinet there lets the past visually compete with the present, which is precisely the confusion the separation exists to end. A panel buries what is, for the journeyman card, the best thing the platform has to say about him. Glance is the register the cabinet actually speaks in: context you take in beside the season without it claiming to be the season. Compact dated lines, year always printed, season honours visually distinct (the Wonder Tag treatment stays theirs alone), and on the season an honour is won it appears in **both** places by design , season honour on the face, dated entry in the cabinet , because "won here" and "held as of here" are both true that year, and the card saying both is the separation working, not a duplicate to deduplicate.

One refusal to write into the Playbook section so it never needs arguing later: no cabinet-derived metrics, ever , no counts in filters, no "most decorated" surfaces, no cabinet size in any payload field the engine or a future tag could read. The cabinet is a record, and the day it becomes a number, the platform has built the trophy-weighted rating it has spent this entire project refusing.
